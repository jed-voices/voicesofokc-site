Episode

Artie Brylev on Work, Family and Battison Honda

VOICES of OKC Wed, 02 Sep 2026 08:26:51 -0500 Show notes from Podbean
YouTube Apple Spotify Podbean
Show notes

Artie Brylev left Russia to escape religious persecution and built a career at Battison Honda. He talks work, family, reputation and making opportunity intentional in Oklahoma City.

Artie grew up in the former Soviet Union, where his religious family practiced under a system that restricted public faith. In New York, $20 in spending money gave him an early reality check. Later, a busboy job at Junior’s gave him a picture of the family life he wanted, while years of work taught him that success without character or trusted relationships does not hold.

With Jed Chappell, Artie talks about leading a family-owned dealership, protecting a reputation, putting family before the office, and why Oklahoma City needs intentional places where young people can encounter mentors and real opportunity.

Content note: Brief, non-graphic references to religious restriction, incarceration, addiction, and youthful legal trouble.

What you’ll hear

- What $20 in spending money taught Artie about expectations
- Why reputation outlasts a single transaction
- How family became a priority rather than an afterthought
- The moment bussing tables changed the life Artie wanted
- Why opportunity for young people should not be left to chance
